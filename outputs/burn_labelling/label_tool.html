Burn labelling: Punjab, Oct–Dec 2022

How to label (read once)
  • Judge only the field under the magenta square in the centre. Each column is one clear Sentinel-2 date (10 m pixels, 400 m box).
  • Top row, true colour: standing paddy is green or golden; harvested stubble is pale yellow-brown; a fresh burn is black or charcoal-grey with sharp field edges, sometimes with smoke nearby; ploughed or sown soil is brown, darker when wet, and wheat shows as a green flush weeks later.
  • Bottom row, SWIR false colour (B12 / B8 / B4): vegetation looks green; dry stubble and bare soil look pink, mauve or brown; a burn scar looks very dark (near-black or deep purple) and appears suddenly between two dates; wet ploughed soil is dark too, but usually browner, more uniform, and darkens gradually or after a field already looked tilled.
  • Choose Burnt and the first date on which the char is visible. If it is ploughed later, it still counts as burnt.
  • Choose Not burnt, ploughed/sown if the field goes from stubble to soil or wheat with no char stage. Use Not burnt, other for no change, standing crop all season, orchard or non-paddy. Use Can't tell when clouds or too few dates make it unclear.
  • The Google Maps link shows recent high-resolution imagery (not 2022). Use it only to check that the spot is a field.
