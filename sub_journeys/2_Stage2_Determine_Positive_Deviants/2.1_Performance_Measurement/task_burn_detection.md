# Burn detection — measuring burning from satellite
**DPPD position:** Stage 2 › Performance Measurement · **Status:** Method locked + validated; tehsil scaling pending · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

## In plain words
To find places that burn less, we first need a reliable way to see burning. NASA fire alerts (FIRMS) miss most small, short paddy fires. So we use Sentinel-2 photos taken a few days after harvest, where burnt fields look dark and charred. We tried three "burn indices" (formulas on the image colours) against 380 fields whose true status was known. The combination of **BAIS2** (sensitive to burnt material) and **TCB** (overall brightness; burnt fields are darker) labelled fields correctly **95.5%** of the time and caught **98%** of burnt fields.

## Technical summary
- **Objective:** a field/pixel-level burnt vs unburnt classifier usable across Punjab.
- **Approaches:**
  - ✘ NASA FIRMS/VIIRS active fire counts: about 3.67× undercount vs Sentinel-2 in Punjab/Haryana.
  - ✘ NBR pre/post change detection: harvest alone causes a large spectral shift that looks like burning.
  - ✘ Simplified two-band BAIS2: implementation bug; gave misleadingly poor results.
  - ✔ Single post-harvest Sentinel-2 snapshot + cropland/paddy mask, **BAIS2 (Filipponi 2018 red-edge formula) + Tasseled Cap Brightness**, logistic-regression decision boundary.
- **Validation data:** `Code/Ground Truth Data/partially_completely_burnt_2021_10_29.geojson` (357 fields; around Jalandhar/Hoshiarpur, bbox 75.48–75.85 E, 31.16–31.43 N) and `..._2020_11_10.geojson` (81 fields; Sangrur-area bbox 75.81–75.93 E, 30.30–30.46 N). Classes: partially_burnt 226, completely_burnt 90, unburnt 95, golden_yellow_unburnt 13, green_unburnt 12. Burnt = partially + completely (316); unburnt = 120. Provenance to confirm before external citation. [?]
- **Pipeline** (`Code/Decision_pilot_ground_data (2).ipynb`, GEE project `ictd-507010`):
  1. `COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED`, closest clear image: 2021 → `20211029T053941_20211029T053942_T43REQ` (cloud ≈0%), covers 357/357 fields; 2020 → `20201110T053041_20201110T053044_T43REP` (cloud 12.7%), covers 79/81 (fields 30 and 40 outside footprint).
  2. Compute NBR, BAIS2, TCB; field-level **median** via `reduceRegions` at 20 m.
  3. Drop fields with missing values: 436 → **380** (265 burnt / 115 unburnt).
  4. Stratified 5-fold CV (`random_state=42`). NBR: best-accuracy threshold learned per fold (≈0.044). BAIS2+TCB: StandardScaler + LogisticRegression per fold.
- **Results (single-index AUC, full data):** NBR 0.883 · BAIS2 0.976 · TCB 0.952.
- **Results (5-fold CV, unseen fields):**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 2026-10-07 — Transferability checks 0–3 (before scaling to all Punjab)
**Plain:** We tested whether the burn model learned on CEEW's fields can be trusted elsewhere.
- (0) The notebook's cloud mask had thrown away nearly all burnt fields on the 2020 date, because Sentinel-2 labels freshly burnt pixels "dark" or "unclassified". So the published 95.5% was really tested almost only on the 2021 Jalandhar fields. A wider mask keeps 436 of 438 fields.
- (1) With the wider mask, a model learned on one date/place works on the other: 100% and 96%.
- (2) At pixel level it is 90% correct, but about 13% of pixels in unburnt fields are wrongly called burnt.
- (3) Across a whole season of images, almost every field eventually looks "burnt" for weeks, including fields CEEW saw unburnt. Some of those truly burnt later, but the model also seems to read freshly ploughed or sown soil as burnt.

So single-date detection is good, but the season-long approach overcounts and needs fixing before we scale.
**Technical:** `Code/burn/transfer_checks.py` (Earth Engine Python API; `init_ee()` falls back to the EE-only OAuth scope because only that consent box was granted) → `outputs/burn_checks/`.
- **Check 0 (SCL mask):** % of field pixels by SCL on label dates. 2021: burnt 98.1% SCL 5, unburnt 89.1% SCL 5 / 10.9% SCL 4. **2020: burnt 32.7% SCL 2 (dark area) + 60.7% SCL 7 (unclassified)**, only 1.3% SCL 5. Notebook mask (SCL 4 + 5) left 380/438 fields: 2020 = 23 fields (20 unburnt / **3 burnt** of 54); 2021 = 357 (95 / 262). Relaxed mask (SCL 2, 4, 5, 7): 436/438 (2020: 25 / 54; 2021: 95 / 262). → The original validation effectively covered one date/area. Use the relaxed mask.
- **Check 1 (transfer, field medians, StandardScaler + logistic):** relaxed mask: train 2021 → test 2020: acc 1.000, recall 1.000, false alarms 0/25; train 2020 → test 2021: acc **0.961**, precision 0.973, recall 0.973, false-alarm rate 0.074. Notebook mask: 2020 → 2021 recall drops to 0.824 (model trained on 3 burnt fields). In-sample 2021: acc 0.958, false-alarm rate 0.105.
- **Check 2 (pixel, pooled model, relaxed):** 436 fields / 8,877 pixels at 20 m: pixel accuracy **0.904**; burnt fields 92.1% of pixels flagged; **unburnt fields 13.0% of pixels flagged**; field majority rule 0.963.
- **Check 3 (multi-look, relaxed, pooled model, CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE < 60):** 2020: 25 images, 2021: 14 images (median 13 looks/field). Season rules: burnt fields detected 100% under any-look / 2-consecutive / became-dark; **unburnt-labelled fields flagged 92.4% / 91.6% / 91.6%**; flagged before their label date 12.6% (n = 119).

- Reading: low false alarms before harvest/burning (≤12%), strong detection at the burn date. ### 2026-10-09 — Visual labelling not feasible; char vs ploughed soil not separable in Sentinel-2
**Plain:** Soham could not tell burnt from ploughed fields in the 10 m chips; only one spot showed clear smoke (5 Nov). So instead we used CEEW's own fields to compare "char" (burnt fields on their label date) with "ploughed soil" (the same fields a month later). They are almost identical in every Sentinel-2 band and index; our main burn index, BAIS2, cannot separate them at all. Char is easy to tell from stubble or green crops, but not from ploughed soil. More features or better models did not fix it, so this is a limit of the data.
**Technical:** `Code/burn/char_vs_soil.py` → `outputs/burn_checks/charsoil_*.csv`. CEEW fields, all clear looks 1 Oct–15 Dec (2020: 25 images, 2021: 14), relaxed SCL mask, field medians at 20 m.
- Proxy classes (field-looks): char 417 (burnt, ±3 d of label), soil 745 (same burnt fields ≥ +25 d, NDVI < 0.3), stubble 95 (unburnt, ±3 d), green 61.
- Medians char / soil / stubble: BAIS2 0.828 / 0.818 / 0.604; TCB 0.294 / 0.322 / 0.468; B12 0.175 / 0.202 / 0.242; NBR2 0.099 / 0.049 / 0.169.
- Single-feature AUC char vs soil: best NBR2 0.733, B12 0.699, B3 0.659, TCB 0.605, **BAIS2 0.511**. Char vs stubble: BAIS2 0.968, B8A 0.966, B8 0.962.
- Cross-year classifiers (logistic, balanced): unstable. E.g. all bands, train 2021 → test 2020: char recall 0.026; train 2020 → 2021: char 0.813, soil called char 0.253.
- Season rules on CEEW fields: no feature set reduces unburnt fields flagged at some look below ~0.82 while keeping detection (BAIS2+TCB: burnt 0.984 / unburnt 0.833; all bands: 0.965 / 0.817; still-flagged-after-25 d 0.862 → 0.242).
- Caveat: the "soil" proxy may retain some char, but the near-identical medians point to a genuine sensor limit at 20 m.

### 2026-10-09 — Literature check: others hit the same wall; how they handled it
**Plain:** We checked whether earlier research faced this. It did. The best Punjab study (Walker et al., with real field checks) found burnt fields look like ploughed fields within 2–3 days, and with Sentinel-2 alone half of the truly unburnt plots were wrongly called burnt. They needed near-daily 3 m PlanetScope images plus ground checks to reach 82% accuracy. A 2025 Sentinel-2 study that ignored ploughing reported "burnt area" equal to 68% of Punjab, which shows the over-count we found. Large policy studies, including a 2024 *Nature* paper, measure burning with NASA's satellite fire detections (MODIS/VIIRS) instead. Those are known to miss fires, but they are consistent across years and places.
**Technical:**
- **Walker, Moscona, Jack, Jayachandran, Kala, Pande, Xue & Burke (2022), "Detecting Crop Burning in India using Satellite Data", arXiv 2209.10148** (code: GitHub burntfields_punjab; data Zenodo 10987987). Ground data 2019, two Punjab districts: 3,206 plots, 441 burnt / 246 confirmed-unburnt (monitor visits + spot checks); plots mean 1.4 ha. Quotes: a plot "may be completely burnt within a day and resemble unburned plots within two to three days"; changes in NIR/SWIR "while marked between pre- and post-burned plots, are similar between pre- and post-tilled plots"; NBR/NDVI-type indices "are not relevant". Random Forest (PlanetScope daily 3 m + Sentinel-2, temporal features): accuracy 0.82, burn 0.91, **no-burn 0.63**; balanced 0.78. **Sentinel-only: no-burn accuracy 0.51** (max-accuracy) / 0.74 (balanced, at the cost of burn accuracy 0.76). The number of Sentinel images is a top feature (more looks → more "burnt" calls), i.e. our accumulation effect. Their S2 cloud masking used s2cloudless rather than SCL, which also drops burn scars.
- **Arbain & Imasu (2025), *Sensors* 25(17):5588, Punjab 2022–24 S2:** dNBR ≥ 0.1 on 2-week composites, ESA WorldCover cropland; no tillage handling; qualitative validation only; peak burnt area > 34,000 km² (~68% of Punjab) vs MODIS < 7,000 km².
- **Naeher & Ziulu (2025), *Applied Economics*, doi 10.1080/00036846.2025.2567582** (in `0. Material/Literature/`): S2 BAIS2 thresholds on pre (29 Sep–5 Oct) vs post (27 Oct–2 Nov 2021) composites, calibrated and validated against VIIRS NPP active fires (375 m) and plot-level survey (Naeher et al. 2023, 1,206 farmers, Punjab + Haryana).
- **Dipoppa & Gulzar (2024), *Nature* 634:1125–1131:** burning measured with MODIS (1 km) + VIIRS (375 m) active fire detections from NASA FIRMS (thermal; not blocked by smoke). **Khanna, Mahajan & RSA (2025, LSE/Ashoka WP):** satellite fire detections 2011–2020 (Giglio et al.) on 10 km grids. VIIRS detects ~4.8× more fires than MODIS over Punjab (Vadrevu & Lasko 2018).
- Known VIIRS biases: afternoon overpass (~13:30) misses later/short fires; small fires missed; Liu et al. (2020) household surveys show gaps in satellite fire records. NOAA-21 VIIRS added from 2023 → use a constant satellite set across 2020–24 (S-NPP + NOAA-20) for comparability.
- Implication [P]: Sentinel-2-only season-long burnt area is not defensible. Options: (1) VIIRS fire counts per tehsil-season / calibrated rice area (precedent: Nature 2024, LSE 2025, CEEW) with an S2 bi-temporal BAIS2 snapshot (Naeher & Ziulu style) as a cross-check, PDs required to hold under both; (2) PlanetScope daily data (Walker) — not feasible in the time available.

### 2026-10-11 — How others set the BAIS2 + TCB boundary; why their ground truth never saw ploughed fields
**Plain:** Earlier studies set the burnt/unburnt line in three ways: fixed cut-offs from histograms (CEEW, Deshpande), a learned line (ours), or a machine-learning model that uses the whole time series (Walker). We tried CEEW's own cut-offs on our ash and ploughed-soil examples: they pass 56% of ash and 57% of ploughed soil, so the same failure. The bigger finding: CEEW deliberately left fields where stubble was mixed into the soil out of their "unburnt" class, because those were hard to recognise. Our ground truth is CEEW's data, so our 96% accuracy was never tested against ploughed fields. That explains why the problem appeared only once we looked across the season.
**Technical:**
- **Percentile thresholds:** Deshpande, Pillai & Jain (2022, *MethodsX* 9:101741): 5th/95th percentiles of index histograms for burnt vs unburnt pixels, BAIS2 and TBI best by separability index. CEEW (2025) follow this without capping: **T3 = BAIS2 ≥ 0.78 and TBI ≤ 0.34** (5th / 95th percentile of partially burnt pixels, Sangrur 10 Nov 2020); pixel accuracy ≈ 75%. Same BAIS2 scale as ours (Filipponi, 0–1 reflectance; their histogram 0–1).
- **Learned boundary:** ours, StandardScaler + logistic regression on field medians.
- **Temporal machine learning:** Walker et al. (2022), Random Forest on PlanetScope + S2 time series; S2-only no-burn accuracy 0.51.
- **Check on our proxy classes** (`charsoil_timeseries.csv`, field-looks passing CEEW T3): char 0.561 (n 417), soil 0.573 (n 745), stubble 0.000 (n 95). → Fails the same way as ours and Naeher & Ziulu's.
- **Key caveat [C, CEEW brief p.12]:** "We excluded in-situ managed fields from the unburnt category, as these were challenging to identify reliably from satellite imagery." The CEEW Zenodo labels (our ground truth) therefore contain no tilled/incorporated unburnt fields; every boundary fitted on them (CEEW's, ours) is untested on the confusing class.
- Leads not yet read [P]: Zhou et al. (2021, *Remote Sensing* 13:5108), Sentinel-2 field-level tillage tracking in smallholder systems; Deshpande et al. (2024, *JAG* 128:103779), Sentinel-2 zero-tillage detection in India. Both attempt to detect tillage directly.

### 2026-10-11 — Tillage papers read: a partial way through, via timing, and a link through Basma
**Plain:** The 2022 burn paper is about wheat fires in Madhya Pradesh and does not deal with ploughing. The 2024 Punjab tillage paper is the useful one. It sorts fields into zero-till (stubble left), Super Seeder (stubble mixed in, no burning) and conventional (stubble cleared, mostly by burning, then ploughed), with about 81% accuracy. The trick is timing: it compares photos from just after harvest, just before sowing and just after sowing. A field whose stubble disappeared *before* the machine came was cleared (mostly burnt); a field that kept its stubble until sowing was not. Even so, they report the same confusion we found: Super Seeder fields look bare like ploughed burnt fields. The tillage index they rely on (NDTI) is the same formula as our NBR2, which separates ash from soil only moderately (0.735). Their 426 surveyed Punjab plots, labelled by tillage type, are the kind of ground truth we lack. Meha Jain, the senior author, co-wrote Basma's 2026 paper, so Basma may be able to connect us.
**Technical:**
- **Deshpande, Pillai & Jain (2022), *JAG* 108:102761:** Madhya Pradesh, post-winter wheat burning, Mar–May 2019–21; monthly S2 composites; training by MODIS-fire-targeted visual sampling; validated with PlanetScope (F1 94%, commission 10.7%). Method details in the MethodsX companion (not in folder). Notes fields "often replanted after a minimum of ten days after an active fire". No tillage discussion. → Not applicable to the char vs tilled-soil problem.

### 2026-10-24 — Soham's idea: "a burnt field should have an unusually high heat peak" — checked with data already in hand
**Plain:** Soham asked (1) how often never-burnt fields get flagged by the heat-peak rule, and (2) whether burnt fields have a much hotter peak than never-burnt ones, so that only "abnormal" peaks would count. ### 2026-10-27 — Correction to "final setting fixed at 0.5": on Walker's ploughed fields that cut calls 51% burnt → two operating points, strict one for DPPD

### 2026-10-27 — Gate test repeated with a 5×5 window and a single pixel: smaller is better; the single pixel catches the most burns, and at the strict cut its false alarms stay low
**Plain:** Soham asked to test a bigger square (5×5, 100 m) and a single 20 m pixel before the tehsil run. The bigger square is clearly worse: at the strict cut it catches only 16 of 63 Planet burns and 36 of 262 CEEW burns. The single pixel is the best of the three: 34 of 63 Planet burns and 102 of 262 CEEW burns. That is even more than the whole-field average on CEEW (90), with false alarms still low (3 of 32 and 2 of 95). The reason is field size: Jalandhar fields are small (half are under 0.42 ha). A 60 m square (0.36 ha) is bigger than about a third of them and a 100 m square (1 ha) is bigger than nearly 9 in 10, so the squares mix in neighbouring fields, bunds and paths, which dilutes the ash. A single pixel stays inside the field, and in a partly burnt field it may land on an ash patch. The cost: at the loose cut (0.5) the single pixel lets in more false alarms (7 of 32, 6 of 95). So the single pixel only works with the strict cut, which is already the main setting.
