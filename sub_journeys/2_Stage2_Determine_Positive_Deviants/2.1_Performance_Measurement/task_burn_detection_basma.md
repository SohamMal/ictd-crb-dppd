## In plain words
Basma suggests a radar check on top of our Sentinel-2 burn detector. Treat each "burnt" flag as a *suspect*, with a date. Then look at Sentinel-1 radar images from just before and just after that date. Radar measures how rough the ground surface is, not its colour. Ploughing turns the soil over and leaves it rough and lumpy, so the radar signal goes **up**. Burning removes the straw but leaves the soil surface as it was, so the signal stays **flat or goes down**. Suspects where the signal jumps up are relabelled as ploughing and dropped. The rest count as burns.

### 2026-10-22 — B5: Basma's radar rule on single fields that were probably ploughed, not burnt — the radar goes the opposite way
**Plain:** Soham wanted the radar idea tested on single fields, not 1 km boxes. Walker's fields have no locations, so we built our own "probably ploughed" set from the CEEW Jalandhar 2021 fields. We took fields the ground team saw as stubble (not burnt) on 29 Oct, then found the 5-day window in which their straw disappeared (Sentinel-2, a clear picture every 5 days). We called a field "probably ploughed" if the ground stayed bright then (brighter than 90% of fresh burns in Walker's ground data) and no fire was detected. Then we checked the radar. Basma's rule says ploughing makes the radar signal **rise**. On the ploughed fields it mostly **fell**. Her rule called only 7% of ploughed fields "ploughing", while it threw out 33% of fields the ground team saw burnt. That is the reverse of what the filter needs. Among the stubble fields, radar barely differs between those ploughed and those burnt later. So radar sees *that* the straw left, not *how*.

Downloaded to `Code/Ground Truth Data/Naeher2023/` (`ESW_data.dta`, despite the .tab name on the server; questionnaire PDF; variables xlsx). 1,206 farmers, 70 villages, 7 districts, fielded Sep–Oct 2022 about Rabi 2021/22. F3 residue fate before wheat sowing: incorporated 633, cattle feed 319, mulch 125, burnt in field 51, burnt after heaping 10, burnt loose only 3 (self-reported; burning ~5%). E9 tillage: cultivator 385, harrow 379, rotavator 185, zero-till 145. F14 rice harvest month + week; F17 ploughings in fallow. GPS for 515 plots (accuracy ~5 m) but published as **village average + uniform noise ±0.1°** (paper's anonymisation), so they are unusable for fields or villages (nearest-neighbour 0.9 km, scattered). Labels with GPS: 23 burnt, 296 incorporated.
- [C] CIMMYT Landscape Diagnostic Survey rice 2018 (hdl 11529/10548656): 388 Punjab + 417 Haryana plots, GPS truncated to 2 decimals, no residue variables named → not useful.
- [C] Jain-lab Bihar tillage (PLOS ONE 2022; Zenodo 6703973 / 6705841, open): Sentinel-1 / Sentinel-2 / Planet values per field sample, labelled zero-till vs conventional, `.geo` empty (no locations), Bihar 2017, no burning or event dates → tests "does S1 see ploughing", not burnt vs ploughed.
- [C] Other searches (Zenodo, Mendeley, Kaggle, PPCB inspections, 2025 S1+S2 Punjab paper): no located field-level burnt + ploughed labels. The 2025 paper trained on visually interpreted S2/VIIRS, not field visits. PPCB inspections are not public at point level.
- [P] Route without waiting: hand-label fields on Google Earth Pro historical VHR around the CEEW dates (Sangrur 2020-11-10, Jalandhar 2021-10-29; also check other Oct–Nov dates in the imagery history). Classes: burnt (black/grey ash), ploughed-not-burnt (furrows, brown, no ash), stubble, sown. Then redo W1 (optical) and B5 (radar) with the event window from the S2 series. Caveat: a burnt-then-ploughed field shows furrows plus grey ash, so mixed cases must be a separate class.
